# Gate R2 conflict: read the conflicting-types line, fix the TU prototype
- An R2 "previous definition" key is often cpp macro-redefinition noise from shared headers. The real error is the "conflicting types" or "too few arguments" line below it.
- A callee declared differently in two TUs of one program fails the program-wide declaration check. Fix the TU-side prototype (declsync), not the body.
- Rewriting a body cannot fix an own-name conflict with the prototypes of the same function in other TUs. It needs a TU-side declsync of those bodies.
- The R2 of a propagated twin can be a callee prototype that a sibling body in the TU spells differently. Fix the TU prototype and keep the call shape.
- A same-TU caller declaration that stops at R2 banks once the caller's declaration is unprototyped, with codegen unchanged.
- A regate whose twin stopped on a caller declaration needs the same caller fix on the twin's callers before the gate.
- An incremental build can pass where the clean rebuild is red. Judge declaration fixes on a clean build.
