# C0001 — The decomp idiom entry shape: residual, mechanism, lever, byte proof
tags: cookbook-format,idiom-shape,residual,mechanism,lever,byte-proof · date: 2026-10-01 · phase/task: - · origin: decomp-architect

Every idiom entry in this cookbook is byte-proven on a named function and carries four parts:

1. **The residual** is what the diff looks like (the tell). It gives the instruction, its position, the register and
   the count.
2. **The mechanism** is the compiler pass that produces the residual, and why, with the dump line that shows it. A
   verdict without a pass and a dump line is a hypothesis.
3. **The lever** is the C-level change that moves the residual, stated as a shape. A register pin is never the answer.
   A pin is a symptom. Before a lever enters the cookbook, strip it from the accepted body and recompile. About one
   credited lever in three is inert.
4. **The byte proof** gives the function, the diff before and after, and the compilation that the claim survived. The
   compilation is standalone, a real translation unit, or whole-binary.

Every entry has the same pinned context. The toolchain triple is `gcc2.95.2-psx-aspsx2.86`
(`docs/ops/compiler-pin.md` in the development repository, phase 1.4). The SDK evidence:
The disc carries "Library Programs (c) 1993-1997 Sony Computer Entertainment Inc." (PsyQ 4.x era).
The executable links PsyQ 4.7 (the same file, "Lib provenance").

No one file records the calling convention and the register roles of the target. `docs/codegen-map/` holds what the
bytes proved.

Tag each entry with its symptom words, so that `grep -i <symptom> cookbook/INDEX.md` finds it. The triage table entry
maps a tell to its mechanism and lever family.
