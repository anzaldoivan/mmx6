# C0087 — Loop guard: while copies its exit test as the guard. A written guard + do-while keeps yours
tags: idiom,sym-loop-reversed,G-jump,jump,loop,guard · date: 2026-10-02 · phase/task: 1.7/T6 · origin: BFM §279 re-proven

1. **Residual (tell)**: the entry guard of a loop is its own exit test (`sltu $2,$4,$3` + `beqz`). At that place, the target guards with a cheaper compare (`blez` on the count), or the reverse. The difference is 2 insns. `sym-loop-reversed`
2. **Mechanism**: jump (G-jump). For a `while`/`for` loop, jump copies the exit test in front of the loop as the guard. It marks the bottom test `NOTE_INSN_LOOP_VTOP`. A `do {} while` has no top test to copy, so the guard is whatever the source writes.
   - src:gcc-2.95.2/gcc/jump.c:341 "if (duplicate_loop_exit_test (insn))"
   - src:gcc-2.95.2/gcc/jump.c:2766 "emit_note_before (NOTE_INSN_LOOP_VTOP, exitcode);"
   - a-side `.jump`: `NOTE_INSN_LOOP_VTOP` is present. The b-side has none.
3. **Lever**: `while (p < e) {...}` gives the copied guard `p < e`. `if (n > 0) do {...} while (p < e);` gives the guard of the source (`blez $5`). The a-side has 12 insns, and the b-side has 10.
